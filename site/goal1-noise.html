The decomposition

Twenty models each forecast 100 markets, in both the market's own phrasing and its negation, four times each. Kahneman's decomposition splits the disagreement in that panel into three parts: level noise (some models are just higher than others), stable pattern noise (a model's consistent idiosyncratic reaction to a particular market), and occasion noise (the same model, the same prompt, a different answer). Case spread is shown beside them because it is the thing the noise competes with — it is the real difference between markets, and it is not noise.

Level noise, by model

Each model's mean forecast minus the grand mean across all models, on the market's Yes scale. Positive means this model is systematically more bullish than the panel; negative, more bearish. One number per model — goal 1.1.

Stable pattern and occasion noise, by model

Stable pattern noise is what survives after averaging the four repetitions — a model's durable idiosyncrasy about specific markets. Occasion noise is the scatter among those four repetitions. The stable-pattern figure has the sampling variance of a four-draw cell mean removed, so a merely inconsistent model does not register as an idiosyncratic one.

Noise against how extreme the market is

The reason every measure here is computed twice. Markets are grouped by their Polymarket midpoint at snapshot time, and within-cell noise is pooled inside each band. Two charts rather than two axes on one: the scales are not comparable and overlaying them would invent a relationship.

Most contested and most irreproducible markets

Two different questions. Between-model spread is genuine disagreement about the world; within-model spread is a model failing to reproduce itself. They do not pick the same markets.
