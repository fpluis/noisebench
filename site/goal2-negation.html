The coherence identity

Every market is asked twice: in its own phrasing ("Will X happen?") and in its negation ("Will X fail to happen?"). Nothing else about the prompt changes — same rules, same research, same model. A coherent forecaster must answer two numbers that sum to 1, whatever it believes. The amount by which the sum misses is the bias, and it needs no ground truth to measure.

Where the incoherence lives

Markets grouped by their Polymarket midpoint at snapshot time. The two bars are what the panel said to each phrasing, as reported. A coherent panel would produce two bars that always sum to 1 — so the pair should cross over as the midpoint passes 0.50, and the orange bar should be tallest at the left edge.

By model

Mean of base + negated − 1 across every market the model answered in both phrasings. Negative is sub-additive: the model spends less than unit mass across a question and its negation.

Which arm moves

The gap is a sum missing its target, so either arm could be the one at fault. Plotting each arm against the market's snapshot midpoint separates them: a well-behaved arm tracks the diagonal.
