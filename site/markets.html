Overview Markets Details

The same two probability-scale measures, sliced by question instead of by model. Both are averages over the twenty models: the average error of one question is how far a typical model's four identical repeats fell from each other on it, and its negation error is how far a typical model's two wordings fell from each other. The market price is what the crowd thought at the moment the question was put to the models — it is context for reading the panel's estimate, never a right answer.

The panel against the market

Each dot is one question. On the diagonal the panel agrees with the market; below it the panel thinks the outcome is less likely than the crowd does, above it more.

Which questions the models were noisiest on

The twenty questions with the largest values, in whichever measure is selected. These are averages over all twenty models, so a question at the top is one the whole field struggled with rather than one model.

Every question

Sorted by market price, longest shots first. Click any heading to sort by it. Model estimate is the panel's own probability for the question — each model's two wordings folded onto one scale, then averaged over models with equal weight — and its shading is how far that sits above (green) or below (red) the market, reaching full strength when the gap reaches 50%. The two rank columns place the question among all —, most likely first, once by market price and once by that estimate: where they differ, the panel and the crowd order the field differently even if the probabilities are close.
