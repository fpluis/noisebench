The coherence identity

Every question is asked twice: in its own wording ("Will X happen?") and in its negation ("Will X fail to happen?"). Nothing else about the prompt changes — same rules, same research, same model. A coherent forecaster must answer two numbers that sum to 100%, whatever it believes. The amount by which the sum misses is the bias, and it needs no ground truth to measure.

Where the incoherence lives

Questions grouped by their market price at the moment they were asked. The two bars are what the panel said to each wording, as reported. A coherent panel would produce two bars that always sum to 100% — so the pair should cross over as the market price passes 50%, and the orange bar should be tallest at the left edge.

By model

Mean of base + negated − 100% across every question the model answered in both wordings. Negative is sub-additive: the model spends less than unit mass across a question and its negation.

Which arm moves

The gap is a sum missing its target, so either arm could be the one at fault. Plotting each arm against the market price when it was asked separates them: a well-behaved arm tracks the diagonal.
