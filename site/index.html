Overview Noise Markets Details

Ask a language model the same forecasting question twice and you get two different answers. Ask it for the opposite outcome and the two answers may not add up. Ask it to rank two outcomes and the ranking may not match the probabilities it just gave. This benchmark measures that — how far a model is from agreeing with itself, never whether it was right. Twenty models, 100 Polymarket questions, 24,000 forecasts.

Noise

One score per model: the unweighted mean of the five failure rates below. Lower is better, and 0% is reachable — a forecaster that repeats itself, keeps its two wordings consistent and ranks the same way twice scores zero on all five. Answering at random would score —.

What goes into the score

Five measures, equally weighted. Two are distances in probability — how far a model's own numbers sit from each other, in percentage points of probability. Three are shares of head-to-head judgments. Each tile shows the field average and the formula it comes from.

Equal weight is not equal contribution: the three judgment shares run several times larger than the two probability distances, so they carry most of the composite. That is why the parts are never shown without the whole.

Where each model gains and loses

The same five measures, per model, lowest first. Repeating yourself turns out to be much easier than staying coherent across wordings, and harder still is matching your own probabilities when the question is put as a ranking.

Negation error, by model

The one measure where the direction of the miss is worth seeing on its own. Every market is asked twice — for the outcome as the market words it, and for the opposite outcome — and a coherent forecaster's two answers must add to 100%. The bars are the absolute error that goes into the score; the table keeps the sign, where positive means the model spends more than 100% across the pair and negative means less.
