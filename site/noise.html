Overview Noise Yes/No bias Details

Twenty models each forecast 100 questions, in the question's own wording and in its negation, four times each. Where does the disagreement in that pile of numbers come from? Three sources, and they mean very different things.

Baseline tilt, by model

How far each model's average forecast sits from the average across all twenty. Positive means this model reads the world as more likely than the field does; negative, less likely. It says nothing about whether the model is right — only that it runs high or low.

Idiosyncrasy and repeat drift, by model

Idiosyncrasy is a model's durable private take on particular questions — the part that survives averaging its four attempts, so it returns every time you ask. Repeat drift is the scatter among those four attempts: same model, same prompt, different answer. The idiosyncrasy figure has the sampling error of a four-attempt average removed, so a model that is merely unsteady is not counted as opinionated.

Noise against how much of a long shot the question is

Questions grouped by their market price at the moment they were asked. The two charts measure the same drift in two units, because the units disagree about what "noisy" means: moving from 1% to 3% is two percentage points but a tripling of the odds.

Which questions did the models disagree about?

Two different failures, easy to confuse. Between-model spread is genuine disagreement — twenty models reading the same question differently. Within-model spread is irreproducibility — one model that cannot give the same answer twice. Both are measured in the units selected at the top of the page, so they are directly comparable to the drift figures above.
